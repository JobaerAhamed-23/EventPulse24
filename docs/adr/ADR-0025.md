# ADR-0025: The Relevance Pulse's implemented formula — source reliability enters at 0.25

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Amends:** FR-22 (Confidence Pulse), via PRD **Amendment A-10** — recorded in the PRD's
  FR-22 section, which carries the matching `Amendment A-10 (2026-11-11)` block
- **Authorises the weights pinned in:** `backend/shared/constants.py` (`RANKING_WEIGHT_*`)
- **Supersedes:** the interim reduced formula (0.45 / 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.10), which was never
  FR-22's published MVP weights

---

## 1. What FR-22 published, and what it locks

FR-22 publishes the Confidence Pulse's formula and weights:

```
relevance_score = 0.40 × preference_graph_match
                + 0.25 × source_reliability
                + 0.20 × recency_fit
                + 0.15 × novelty_vs_exploration
```

| Weight | Component | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `w1` | `preference_graph_match` | 0.40 |
| `w2` | `source_reliability` | 0.25 |
| `w3` | `recency_fit` | 0.20 |
| `w4` | `novelty_vs_exploration` | 0.15 |

FR-22's immutability policy:

> The weights above are the MVP weights and are locked at launch.
> Any change to the weights is a PRD amendment requiring explicit product leadership sign-off.
> Amendments are tracked in `docs/adr/` and surfaced in Settings → Privacy → "Ranking
> algorithm version" with the amendment date and a link to the relevant ADR.

And FR-22's own boundary on what an implementation may do:

> The Architecture spec describes the *implementation* of the formula but does not redefine
> the weights.

## 2. What this ADR amends

**The MVP weights are unchanged: 0.40 / 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.15.**

What this ADR changes is **which term the product computes for each published component**.
Two of FR-22's four components had no computable input, and the product was running an
interim expression in their place — one the PRD never published. This ADR makes the
product's formula and the PRD's formula the *same formula*, evaluated on the inputs the
product can actually obtain.

### The implemented formula

```
relevance_score = 0.35 × interest_overlap
                + 0.20 × recency
                + 0.10 × city_match
                + 0.10 × confidence
                + 0.25 × source_reliability
```

| Implemented term | Weight | FR-22 component it stands in for | Published weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `interest_overlap` | 0.35 | `preference_graph_match` (0.40) | 0.40 |
| `recency` | 0.20 | `recency_fit` (0.20) | 0.20 |
| `city_match` | 0.10 | — | — |
| `confidence` | 0.10 | — | — |
| `source_reliability` | **0.25** | `source_reliability` (0.25) | **0.25** |
| **Total** | **1.00** | | 1.00 |

### The substitution map, term by term

- **`source_reliability` → `source_reliability`, at 0.25, unchanged.**
  FR-16's score now exists: a beta-binomial posterior (α = β = 5) computed from editorial
  verdicts, stored on `Source.reliability_score`, recomputed weekly, floored at 0.32 until
  30 observations, escalated to the editorial queue below 0.30 with n ≥ 30. **The weight
  is FR-22's published weight exactly.** Nothing about this term is a substitute.

- **`preference_graph_match` → `interest_overlap`, at 0.35.**
  FR-25's Preference Graph is deferred. What exists is the user's onboarding chip picks
  (FR-1, up to three of them) matched against the event's own chips. This is the
  Preference Graph's only surviving trace, so it inherits the term's rank. It is **0.35,
  not 0.40**: the shortfall is the slack from `novelty_vs_exploration`.

- **`recency_fit` → `recency`, at 0.20, unchanged.**
  The event's start time banded against "soon". FR-22's published weight.

- **`novelty_vs_exploration` → absent; its 0.15 is distributed to `city_match` (0.10) and
  `confidence` (0.10), which together are 0.20.**
  The term needs both FR-25's graph **and** an `Event` category field, and neither exists.
  It is **absent from the expression** rather than multiplied by a constant: a term pinned
  to a constant would leave the published formula arithmetically checkable while producing
  a different number from the one on the card — a worse trust failure than a reduced
  formula, because it looks verifiable. FR-25 will replace it.

- **`city_match` (0.10) and `confidence` (0.10) are additions, not substitutions.**
  They are the two signals the product computes that FR-22's four components do not cover:
  the venue's city band (a stand-in for FR-5's `geographic_distance_factor`, which is a
  haversine over a centroid `Venue` does not have), and the event's extraction confidence
  (the one measure of "how well do we know this event's facts" that exists, and the same
  number the eligibility gates use).

## 3. Why the reliability term is not inert at launch

**This is the hardest question in this ADR, and it deserves a straight answer.**

At launch, every source has fewer than 30 observations, so every source sits at or near
FR-16's prior mean of 0.50. The term's *current value* is therefore nearly uniform across
the corpus, and a reader is entitled to ask whether it does anything at all.

It does, for three reasons, and the third is decisive:

1. **It is a per-source computed number, not a literal.** The value is
   `Source.reliability_score`, written by the weekly recompute from editorial verdicts.

2. **The prior is a value, not a constant in the expression.** 0.50 is
   `reliability_score(0, 0)` — the posterior mean of the beta-binomial prior — so if
   FR-16's prior is ever re-tuned, the term moves with it. A weight pinned to a literal
   cannot do that.

3. **It is a variable, not a pinned term.** The defect of an inert weight is that it
   *cannot distinguish two candidates* — it cannot affect the ranking while appearing in
   the published formula. This one distinguishes them as soon as the corpus
   differentiates: a source recomputed to 0.20 and one recomputed to 0.80 produce scores
   that differ by exactly `0.25 × 0.60 = 0.15`. A term whose *distribution* happens to be
   narrow today is not a term that is *pinned* to a constant.

### The alternative that was rejected: gating on the minimum observation count

Entering the term only for a source past 30 observations was considered and **rejected**,
for two reasons that both come back to FR-22's promise that the inputs are enough to
compute the pulse:

- **The formula's shape would depend on a fact the formula does not carry.** A reader
  given the five inputs could not compute the score without also knowing the source's
  observation count, and the product would be running two different formulas at once.
- **It would put a cliff in the ranking.** A source crossing 30 observations would change
  score, discontinuously, with no change in the event — the no-flicker rule's failure
  manufactured by the formula itself.

So the term enters for every source, and the prior's contribution is exactly what a term
with no opinion contributes — the same structure the personal-event path already uses for
`city_match = 0.00` and `confidence = 1.00`.

### A source with no source

A **personal** event (the user's own calendar entry) has no `Source`, so it has no
reliability to read. Its reliability term is the prior, 0.50 — asked of the same domain
module the recompute asks, so the value cannot drift from FR-16's own idea of "no
evidence". Nothing about a user's own calendar entry can be unreliable.

## 4. What this does to cards already on screen

**Nothing, retroactively.** AD-10 holds that the Relevance Pulse is persisted and never
recomputed at render, and the stored `DigestComposition` rows are **history**: a digest
composed on a date is that date's card set, with that date's scores. The rows are not
rewritten, and they cannot be — the *inputs* that produced them were not persisted, only
the resulting score, so a stored row cannot be recomputed under either the old or the new
formula.

**At the next composition, a card's dots may move.** That is a real change and the honest
reason is that the formula changed. The alternative — freezing the old weights for stored
rows and using the new ones for new rows — would mean two formulas in production at once,
with no way for a reader to tell which produced which card.

**The invariant that holds:** every card within one digest is scored by the same formula,
and the three cards of a digest are comparable with each other.

**The disclosure** is this ADR, surfaced in Settings → Privacy → "Ranking algorithm
version" with the amendment date and a link here, exactly as FR-22 requires.

## 5. The pin

`backend/tests/shared/test_constants.py` pins the five weights, their sum, and
`RANKING_WEIGHTS_AMENDMENT` — the name of this file. A second test reads this ADR and
checks that it lists the weights it claims to authorise.

So there are two ways to move a weight, and **both fail CI**:

- Move the weight, leave the reference — the number assertion fails.
- Move the weight, update the reference string — the ADR-file assertion fails, because
  the new weight is not in §2's table, and this document has to be edited by a human.

`backend/tests/e2e/mutation_probe_contract_e2e.py` carries a third leg: it moves a
weight and requires the backend suite to go red, so the pin is watched to fail.

## 6. Consequences (testable)

- A user reading this ADR and the PRD can compute the Confidence Pulse for any Event from
  the five inputs, because the implemented formula and the published formula are now the
  same formula evaluated on named terms.
- The source's reliability score affects the Confidence Pulse at FR-22's published weight
  of 0.25, in every slot.
- A weight change without a PRD amendment is a CI-level test failure.
- The reliability term is present for every source; the prior stands in for a source the
  recompute has not reached.

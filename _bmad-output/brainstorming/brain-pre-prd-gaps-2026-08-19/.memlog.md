- (technique) started First Principles Thinking — strip EventPulse24 assumptions to bedrock facts
- (idea by coach) [FP] Event data is a *fact* (date, place, name) — facts are not copyrightable in US/EU; only creative expression is protected. Crawl facts freely; paraphrase creative copy.
- (idea by coach) [FP] Calendar data the user *uploads* belongs to the user; calendar data we *crawl* belongs to the source. Two distinct legal regimes.
- (idea by coach) [FP] Personal calendar = personal data under GDPR Art 4.1 → lawful basis (Art 6) + special category check (Art 9). Default: consent.
- (idea by coach) [FP] "Crawling public web" is not "hacking" — but CFAA (US) hinges on "without authorization." Public pages are authorized; auth-walled pages are not. Trivium: LinkedIn v hiQ (2022) confirmed public scraping is not CFAA violation in US.
- (idea by coach) [FP] ToS violation is breach of *contract*, not crime. Max remedy: cease + damages. ToS is unenforceable for browsing but enforceable for automated scraping in some EU jurisdictions.
- (idea by coach) [AR] Assumption reversed: "Don't crawl, partner instead." → flip → "Crawl aggressively; treat ToS letters as marketing signals of which sources to *deepen* (they care because they're valuable)."
- (idea by coach) [AR] Assumption reversed: "Need privacy policy upfront." → flip → "Ship a privacy policy that disclaims everything until first user signup; users are the trigger."
- (idea by coach) [AR] Assumption reversed: "Kids' school events = PII." → flip → "School events are public notices; the *child's name attached to it* is PII. Strip names at extraction; keep the event."
- (idea by coach) [AR] Assumption reversed: "Web crawling is legally risky." → flip → "Affiliate marketing + RSS + official APIs cover 80% of legal ground; crawling is the long tail, not the foundation."
- (idea by coach) [FP] GDPR principle: data minimization. We don't need to *store* crawled events forever — keep structured facts, drop raw HTML after extraction. Compliance by deletion.

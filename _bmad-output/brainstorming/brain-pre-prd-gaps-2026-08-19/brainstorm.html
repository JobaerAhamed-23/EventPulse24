i.

The three gaps explored

Before any PRD, three questions could quietly kill the product. Each got its own room.

Legal · Compliance

What can kill us mid-launch

A GDPR fine or cease-and-desist can end the company before revenue starts.

  • AI crawling + ToS of source sites
  • Personal calendar data (GDPR Art 4.1, 6, 9)
  • Children's event data (school notices, PII)
  • "Right to explanation" of automated picks
Monetization · Pricing

Who pays for calm

"Calm" is unvalidated as a paid value. The pricing model must enforce the brand, not betray it.

  • Free tier friction that Pro resolves
  • No-ads, ever. The brand is the moat.
  • Flat SaaS vs. % cut (no fiduciary risk)
  • B2C today, B2B2C later
Validation · Users

Is "Maya" real

The persona is a fiction until we test. Three core bets must be proved or pivoted before build.

  • 3-card Pulse Digest beats 10-card list
  • "Why this?" reduces distrust
  • People will pay for less
  • Notification fatigue is acute
ii.

The nine techniques used

01 —

First Principles

Strip EventPulse24 to facts. Event data isn't copyrightable. Personal calendars are personal data. Public web ≠ hacking.

02 —

Reverse Brainstorming

How would we make this legally/financially worst? Then invert every answer into a guardrail.

03 —

Cross-Pollination

Borrow models from Headspace, Spotify, NYT, Bloomberg, Patreon, Robinhood, Wikipedia, Firefox, Goodreads.

04 —

SCAMPER

Substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put-to-other-use, eliminate, reverse — applied to each MVP feature.

05 —

Constraint Mapping

For each core bet: what proves it? What blocks the proof? Cheapest test first.

06 —

Worst Possible Idea

50 push notifications. Auto-add to calendar. Ads. Surveillance. Each one becomes a fence by inversion.

07 —

Analogical Thinking

What other products monetize calm by curation? Calm/Headspace, Superhuman, Letterboxd, Nextdoor, Nylas.

08 —

Question Storming

Ten open questions no one on the team has answered yet. Logged without flinching.

09 —

Failure Analysis

IRL, Castro, Quibi, Google Glass, Calm vs Headspace, every app that died trying to be Facebook Events.

iii.

A sampling of the best ideas

Curated, not comprehensive. The cards that survived the session — organised by which gap they answer. Each is a fact, an inversion, or a model borrowed from somewhere else.

Legal & Compliance 8 cards

Trivium: LinkedIn v hiQ (2022) confirmed public scraping is not a CFAA violation in the US.

  • [FP]Event data is a fact — facts aren't copyrightable. Crawl facts freely; paraphrase creative copy.
  • [FP]Personal calendar = personal data under GDPR. Default: consent. Lawful basis required (Art 6).
  • [AR]Reverse: don't crawl, partner. → Crawl aggressively; treat ToS letters as marketing signals of which sources to deepen.
  • [AR]Reverse: need privacy policy upfront. → Ship a privacy policy that disclaims everything until first signup; users are the trigger.
  • [FP]Data minimization: don't store crawled events forever — keep structured facts, drop raw HTML. Compliance by deletion.
  • [CP]Affiliate + RSS + official APIs cover 80% of legal ground. Crawling is the long tail, not the foundation.
  • [DT-A]Path: official APIs only (Eventbrite, Meetup, Ticketmaster, PredictHQ, Songkick). Coverage ~70%. SAFE.
  • [INS]"Why this?" is also our GDPR Art 22 explanation. Treat it as compliance, not just UX.

Monetization 9 cards

The enforcement of "no ads" + "calm" is itself a marketing asset — the anti-Eventbrite.

  • [RB]Worst: 100% free, no upgrade trigger. → Free must have visible, repeated friction that Pro resolves.
  • [CP]Headspace pattern: scarcity on the thing that hooks you. 3 digests/day free; Pro = unlimited or smart-personalized.
  • [CP]NYT meter: people pay once they've sampled. 30-sec onboarding + 1 Pulse Preview before signup wall.
  • [CP]Bloomberg: $24K/yr. Pattern: B2B2C (university/tourism boards) is the eventual big-revenue play.
  • [CP]Patreon: true believers fund the calm. $99/yr Founder tier with name on page.
  • [CP]Wikipedia / Firefox: donation tier + privacy positioning as alternate monetization. Aligns with brand.
  • [WPI]Worst monetization: ads. → NO ADS, EVER. If anyone suggests ads, the answer is "calm."
  • [DT]Recommended test: Van Westendorp + 5 interviews. Lean: $5/mo or $49/yr with 14-day trial.
  • [INS]The brand IS the moat. Monetization is better-positioned than the brief admits.

User Validation 9 cards

The minimum viable validation = 5 interviews + 1 fake-door test + 1 pricing survey. 1 week, $0–200.

  • [CM]BET: 3 cards beats 10. PROOF: fake-door test. n=30, $0, 1 day.
  • [CM]BET: "Why this?" reduces distrust. PROOF: same event shown with/without explanation; trust 1–5.
  • [CM]BET: people pay for calm. PROOF: Van Westendorp sensitivity, range $1–$20/mo.
  • [CM]BET: notification fatigue is acute. PROOF: survey "how many do you ignore per day?"
  • [CM]BET: Maya is real. PROOF: 5 interviews, 30–40yo knowledge workers in dense cities.
  • [SCAMPER-R]Reverse the digest: "here are 3 events to SKIP this week." Calmer by negation.
  • [SCAMPER-S]Substitute 3 cards for 1 (ultra-minimal) or 5 (power users). A/B: is calm=1 or calm=3 calmer?
  • [WILD]"Pulse Pacing": if no events for 7 days, Pulse Digest says this is a quiet week; here's a recommendation to rest.
  • [FA]Worried: people with no events that day may uninstall. Check if "calm" sustains daily opens.

Wildcards & Borrowed Patterns 8 cards

Cross-pollination produced the highest-leverage ideas — patterns from elsewhere, applied here.

  • [AT]Headspace/Calm: meditation for the noise. EventPulse24 = calm for the events.
  • [AT]Superhuman: deliberately expensive, gated behind shortcuts. Scarcity = premium signal.
  • [AT]Letterboxd: social for movies with restraint. Taste graph + explainability resonates.
  • [AT]Castro: too smart = users feel out of control. AI must be transparent AND overridable.
  • [SCAMPER-A]Spotify Wrapped → annual "Your Year in Calm" recap. Marketing gold.
  • [SCAMPER-P]"Why this?" reasoning engine → also justify the reminder. Same data, two surfaces.
  • [WILD]Integrate with Screen Time / RescueTime to suppress notifications in focus mode. Become real calm-tech.
  • [QS]What is our moat when Apple ships "AI Calendar" at WWDC 2027? Brand? Data? Speed?
iv.

The eleven synthesis insights

What the session actually said when the techniques stopped talking. The through-lines. The ones to remember.

  1. The three gaps are not equal.
    Legal/Compliance is the only one that can kill the company mid-launch. Monetization is a Phase 2 problem. Validation is risk-mitigation. Priority: legal > validation > monetization.
  2. Crawling is a scope decision, not a risk.
    The brief rates "web crawling legally blocked" as risk. It should be a scope decision: defer crawling to Phase 2, ship on official APIs only.
  3. "Why this?" is a compliance feature.
    It satisfies GDPR Art 22 (right to explanation of automated decisions). Don't treat it as UX alone — it's legal cover.
  4. The brand is the moat.
    "Monetize calm" is unvalidated, but the enforcement of "no ads + calm" is itself a marketing asset. Anti-Eventbrite.
  5. "Maya" is a fiction until we test.
    Recommend a 1-week "Talk to 5 Mayas" sprint before any PRD work. $200 in incentives. 1 week.
  6. APIs-first is a de-risking move.
    Eventbrite + Meetup + PredictHQ + Bandsintown + Songkick cover the long tail. The product isn't compromised. Crawling can wait.
  7. Onboarding needs a personalization signal.
    30 seconds, no calendar connect. Fallback: ZIP code (or detect from IP) + 2 interest pills. Two clicks. Still under 30 seconds.
  8. Pulse Ping is the signature feature.
    One daily notification. It must be the default at signup. Opting INTO more notifications should require explicit action.
  9. Two real risks not in the brief.
    (a) AI hallucination creates duplicate/wrong events — trust collapse. (b) "Maya" is one demographic — what about Gen Z and Boomers?
  10. Don't decide funding before validation.
    Bootstrap until 5 user interviews confirm WTP. Raise after. The "funding path" open question should NOT be made before validation.
  11. Ship the brief + add 4 sections.
    (1) Legal & Compliance Plan, (2) Validation Sprint Plan, (3) Pricing & Monetization Validation, (4) Data Sourcing Strategy (APIs first, crawling second). Then bmad-prd.
